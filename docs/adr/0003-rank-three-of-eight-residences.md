# Housing ranks three residences out of eight

The client asked for Housing to feel like Booking, and Booking is a
single-choice interface — so a future reader will reasonably wonder why this
screen asks for a ranked shortlist instead. Two reasons: a U.S. university does
not guarantee the room a student asks for, so single choice would make a promise
the institution cannot keep; and the catalogue grows from three residences to
eight, which is what makes ranking a real narrowing rather than the theatre it
was when the student ranked three options out of three.

## Consequences

- The Booking resemblance is carried by the **card**, not by the selection model:
  inline photo carousel, amenity chips, filter pills. The student browses like
  Booking and commits like a housing office.
- Eight residences means roughly 24 curated images. These come from Unsplash
  under free licence, downloaded locally with per-file credit in
  `public/images/CREDITS.md`, matching how the first nine were sourced.
- Cost per semester is deliberately **not** shown. With a price on each card the
  student compares spreadsheets; without it they compare places to live. The
  deposit has its own step.
- Off campus stops being half of this step, per the client's request, but a
  discreet "I'll arrange my own" exit remains so a student who already lives in
  the city is not left without a path.
