# Three Phases and a Closing, not seven flat steps

The flow presented seven equally-weighted steps in one list, which read as an
undifferentiated block of work and made the rail the heaviest thing on screen.
We group them into three Phases — _Deciding_, _About you_, _Your life on
campus_ — and pull Review & sign and Deposit out of the count entirely as the
**Closing**, on the grounds that a Phase is where the student *gives*
information and the Closing is where they *confirm* what they already gave.

## Considered Options

- **Keep seven flat steps, fix each screen individually.** Rejected: the review
  call's complaint was about sequence and weight, not about any one screen, and
  four rounds of per-screen fixes had already failed to move it.
- **A continuous flow with no named steps.** Rejected: a twenty-minute form with
  no waypoints gives the student no way to judge what is left or to stop and
  resume.

## Consequences

- Three is also the number that fits a segmented mobile progress bar, which is
  what made mobile-first affordable at all.
- Step **About you** had to be renamed **Identity & contact**, because the Phase
  took its name. A Phase can be described loosely; a form cannot.
- Time estimates and optional/required labels live on the Phase in the rail, not
  on Review & sign where the client originally asked for them. This shows the
  information for the whole flow rather than only at the end — more than was
  asked for, not less, and the client should be told that explicitly.
